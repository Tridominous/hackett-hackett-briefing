The distinction that keeps getting lost

Fourteen is the number of divisions. Nineteen is the number of named partners. They are not competing versions of the same count. A division is a line of work the group sells; a partner is a named firm that delivers some of it. Nineteen partners sit against seven of the fourteen divisions; the other seven are delivered in house. Any document that implies the group has nineteen divisions, or fourteen partners, is wrong — and a journalist or buyer who checks will find that in a minute.

Where the current materials disagree

These are the live inconsistencies a brand standard exists to close. None are difficult; all of them are visible to anyone checking.

  • Service count. Thirteen on the onboarding deck (security omitted), fourteen on the website, fifteen in the internal study. Fourteen is correct.
  • Telephone. Three different numbers across the site and internal documents. One number, published everywhere.
  • Websites. Two live public sites for one brand. One canonical domain, with the other redirecting.
  • Email. Team members using personal accounts alongside company addresses. One domain, one convention.
  • Mission statement. The deck's wording describes a different company that shares the Hackett name. Replace with the group's own language.

Logo

The mark as it currently exists, shown on the three backgrounds it has to survive. The recommendation here is mostly about file formats, because that is what is actually blocking consistent use.

On white — the primary lockup.
HACKETT & HACKETTInternational Group
On near-black — needs a light or magenta version. A JPEG cannot do this.
HACKETT & HACKETTInternational Group
On brand magenta — needs a pure white version.
The blocking issue

The only master currently in circulation is a JPEG with a baked-in white background, served from the website's media host at a fixed 980 × 162. That format cannot sit on a dark header, cannot be printed at scale, cannot be given to a venue or a journalist, and cannot be placed on the magenta itself. Everything else in this section is easy once that is fixed.

What to commission

  • An SVG master, plus transparent PNG at 1×/2×/3×. These are the two formats every request will ask for.
  • Three colourways — full colour on light, all-white for dark and magenta backgrounds, and single-colour black for fax-grade reproduction such as embroidery, etching and single-ink print.
  • A stacked variant for square placements: social avatars, app icons, stamps on vehicle livery.
  • A favicon set derived from the same master, so the browser tab matches the letterhead.

Usage rules

  • Clear space. Keep free space on all sides equal to the height of the ampersand. Nothing crosses it — no text, no rule, no photograph edge.
  • Minimum size. 120 px wide on screen, 30 mm wide in print. Below that, use the stacked variant.
  • Placement on photography. Only over an area of even tone. If the image is busy, put the logo on a solid block rather than fighting the picture.
  • Never stretch, rotate, recolour outside the three approved colourways, add effects, or rebuild the wordmark by typing it in a similar font.

The one rule that protects the magenta

It is a signature colour, not a body colour. On a page, it should appear in one or two places: the mark, and one deliberate emphasis. When magenta is used for headings, links, buttons, rules and highlights all at once, it stops signalling anything. Let the near-black carry the bulk of the page and reserve the magenta for what should be noticed first.

Contrast

Every pairing below is measured in the browser as this page loads, against the WCAG 2.1 formula. No ratio below is asserted from memory; each is computed from the hex values in section 3.

PairingSampleRatioNormal textLarge text & UI
How to read this

WCAG AA asks for 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (roughly 24 px, or 19 px bold) and for interface components. AAA asks for 7:1. The practical consequence for this brand: the signature magenta is comfortable for headings, buttons and the logo, but not for body-size text, which is the reason Magenta Deep and Magenta Light exist.

One pairing to watch

Hackett Magenta on Hackett Black measures 4.42:1 — it clears the 3:1 bar for large text and interface elements, but sits just under the 4.5:1 needed for body copy. This matters because it is the group's most characteristic pairing: magenta on near-black is the look of the group structure document itself. The remedy is not to abandon the pairing, but to limit it: keep magenta on black for headings, rules, the mark and large figures, and set body copy in Magenta Light (#E85EA0), which measures 6.24:1 on the same background and looks like the same colour at text size.

Accessibility here is not only a legal or ethical position. Corporate and public-sector buyers increasingly ask for a statement of conformance during procurement, and a group that already meets AA can answer that question in one line instead of starting a project.

Typography

A serif for authority and a grotesque for clarity. Both are open-licence, so there is no per-seat cost, no licence to renew, and nothing to stop a partner or printer using them correctly.

Display & headings — Fraunces, 600

Extravagantly extraordinary, every time

Subheadings — Fraunces, 600

Fourteen divisions, nineteen named partners, one standard

Body — Hanken Grotesk, 400 / 1.75

The valuable position is not owning cars, jets or venues, but being the one number a client calls, holding the relationship, and knowing who to trust with each job. That is built from access and judgement, which is what makes it difficult for a competitor to copy.

Eyebrow & label — Hanken Grotesk, 700, tracked

Established 2009 · London W1W

Scale and setting

  • Line length. 60–70 characters. Long measures are the most common reason a well-typeset page still feels hard to read.
  • Line height. 1.7–1.8 for body, 1.2 for headings.
  • Weights. Two only — 400 and 600/700. A third weight adds no meaning and costs load time.
  • Substitutes. Where the fonts cannot be installed — email signatures, Word templates, a partner's system — use Georgia for the serif and Segoe UI or Helvetica for the sans. Specify these in the template so nobody improvises.
  • Never set body copy in the display serif, and never letter-space lowercase text.

Voice

How the group should sound in writing. The tone follows from what the business actually sells, which is discretion.

The register

Assured, specific, unhurried. The reader is usually an assistant, a household manager or a private office — someone competent and short of time. Write for them: what it is, what it costs, what happens next.

Confidence in this market reads as restraint. Buyers respond better to a listed certification than to a string of adjectives.

The discipline

Claim only what can be checked. Every superlative invites verification, and verification is where a premium brand is most exposed. Where a partner delivers, say so — "delivered with our certified partners" is a stronger sentence than an unevidenced in-house claim, and it has the advantage of being true.

Do

  • Name the standard. "SIA-licensed close protection" beats "the very best in security."
  • Give the number. Seventeen years, 4.6★ across 49 reviews, 100+ countries.
  • Attribute delivery honestly. "Arranged through our aviation partner."
  • Answer the practical question. Response times, vetting, insurance, what happens if a flight moves.
  • Write short sentences. They read as certainty.

Don't

  • Name clients, households or venues — including by strong implication. This is the rule most often broken by accident, and the most damaging to break.
  • Use "royal", "celebrity" or "VIP" as proof. Unverifiable, and it trades on the discretion the client is paying for.
  • Stack adjectives. "Bespoke luxury premium elite" describes nothing.
  • Advertise a division with no evidence behind it. An unevidenced claim costs more credibility than the line earns.
  • Improvise the boilerplate. Use section 8.
On discretion, specifically

The founder's access is the group's most valuable asset and the one that marketing can most easily destroy. A case study that a knowledgeable reader can decode is a breach, whether or not a name appears. When in doubt, describe the problem solved and the standard applied, never the client. In this market reputation is carried by introduction rather than by published work.

Boilerplate

Three approved descriptions. Every claim in them is checkable against Companies House, the group structure or published reviews. Copy them rather than writing new ones.

One line — 22 words

Hackett & Hackett International Group is a London-based lifestyle management group, established in 2009, providing chauffeur, security, events and private staffing services worldwide.

Short — 58 words

Hackett & Hackett International Group Limited is a London-based lifestyle management group, established in 2009 and based in Great Portland Street, W1W. It provides chauffeur and ground transport, SIA-licensed close protection, event management and production, weddings, household staffing and concierge services across fourteen divisions, delivered directly and with a network of nineteen named partners in over 100 countries.

Full — 118 words

Hackett & Hackett International Group Limited (Companies House 07100605) is a London-based lifestyle management group founded in 2009 by Edwin Hackett, operating from Great Portland Street, W1W. The group works across fourteen service divisions — including chauffeur and ground transport, SIA-licensed security and close protection, event management and production, weddings, film production support, household staffing and concierge — delivered directly and through a network of nineteen named delivery partners covering more than 100 countries. The model is deliberately asset-light: the group holds the client relationship and selects who delivers each job. It supports five charities, including Missing People, Amnesty International and the Film + TV Charity. Most new work arrives by personal recommendation.

Two words to retire

"International Group" is accurate as a legal name and is fine in the boilerplate. It becomes a liability when paired with claims the filings do not support. Until the accounts show it, avoid pairing the name with language implying scale — "global operation", "worldwide offices", "our teams around the world". The group's genuine strength is a London firm with international reach through named partners, which is the more defensible claim.

Press Contact & Assets

What a journalist, venue, partner or procurement team should be able to get without asking twice.

The single point of contact

One named person owns press, one address receives it, and it is published on the site. A press enquiry landing in a shared operational inbox is the most common way a good story is lost.

Recommended: press@ on the canonical domain, forwarding to the named owner, with a stated response time of one working day.

Photography

Photography is the one asset that cannot be borrowed. Stock imagery is recognisable to the buyers this group most wants, and it undercuts a discretion-led promise. One day of real photography — vehicles, a protection officer, an event build, the London street — replaces every stock image across both sites and the whole asset pack, which makes it the highest-return item on this list.

Scope

None of this changes what the business does or how a client reaches it. These are recommendations about consistency: what makes fourteen divisions, nineteen partners and one founder read as one company from the outside. They are offered for discussion, not as decisions taken.
